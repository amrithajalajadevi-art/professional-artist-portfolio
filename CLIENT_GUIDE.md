## 3. Managing Home Page Content & Hero Banners

The top section of your homepage creates the first impression for visitors, curators, and collectors.

### How to Update the Headline & Hero Image:
1. In the left navigation, click on **Home Page Content**.
2. Click on the document titled **Home Page Content**.
3. Under **Flexible Hero Section**, you have complete control over:

   - **Headline Statement**: Change the prominent introductory title displayed across the top of your homepage (e.g., *"Contemporary Figurative Painting & Public Murals"*).
   
   - **Hero Image Source**: Choose how you want to pick the main image:
     - **Option A — Project Reference** *(Recommended)*: Choose an existing painting or mural from your collection. Click the dropdown or search bar under **Project Reference** and select the artwork you want to showcase. The website will automatically pull its high-resolution image and title.
     - **Option B — Custom Image Upload**: Select **Custom Image Upload** if you wish to upload a unique banner photograph that isn't part of your artwork catalogue. An image upload box will appear.
   
   - **Custom Mural Split Images (For Infinite Marquee)**:
     - If you want an animated scrolling marquee of mural details, click **Add item** under this field and upload multiple wide shots or close-up detail photos.
     - The homepage will display them as a continuous, auto-scrolling visual reel.

4. When you are satisfied with your changes, click the green **Publish** button at the bottom right.

## 4. Managing Artworks & Figurative Paintings

The **Artworks** section powers your main gallery pages, category filters, and detail popups.

### Adding a New Painting:
1. In the left menu, click **Artworks**.
2. Click the **pencil / create icon** (`+`) at the top of the middle column.
3. Fill in the artwork details:
   - **Title**: Enter the name of the artwork (e.g., *"Veiled Contemplation"*).
   - **Slug**: Click the **Generate** button next to the slug field. This automatically creates a clean, web-safe link for this piece (e.g., `veiled-contemplation`).
   - **Category**: Select the appropriate series from the dropdown menu:
     - *Rest & Quietude Series*
     - *Recent Figurative Paintings*
     - *Commission works*
     - *Studio Practice*
     - *Drawings & Paper Works*
   - **Medium**: Type the artistic materials used (e.g., *"Oil and raw pigment on Belgian linen"*).
   - **Year**: The creation year (e.g., `2026`).
   - **Dimensions / Scale**: (Optional) Dimensions of the canvas (e.g., *"150 × 120 cm"*).
   - **Location / Collection**: (Optional) Where the piece currently resides (e.g., *"Private Collection, London"* or *"Artist Studio"*).
   - **Images Gallery**: Click **Upload** or drag-and-drop your high-resolution painting photograph here.
   - **Alternative Text**: Briefly describe what is in the painting for screen readers and search engines (e.g., *"Figurative oil painting showing a woman seated in warm ochre light"*).
   - **Description**: Add curatorial notes, conceptual background, or poetic context for the piece.
   - **Featured on Homepage**: Toggle this switch to **On** if you want this painting to be highlighted in the featured grid on your homepage.
4. Click **Publish** to make your new artwork visible to the public!

### Editing or Removing an Artwork:
- **To Edit**: Click the artwork from the list, update any text or photo, and click **Publish**.
- **To Remove**: Click the artwork, click the three small dots (`...`) in the bottom right corner next to the Publish button, and select **Delete**.

## 5. Mastering the Hotspot & Crop Tool (Perfect Portraits)

Because visitors view your website on devices with vastly different screen shapes (tall smartphone screens, square tablets, and ultra-wide laptop monitors), your website uses an intelligent **Focal Point (Hotspot)** tool to guarantee that faces and key details are never awkwardly cut off.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                  IMAGE CROP TOOL             │
│                                              │
│     ┌──────────────────────────────────┐     │
│     │               ○  <── HOTSPOT DOT │     │
│     │            (Face)                │     │
│     │                                  │     │
│     │                                  │     │
│     │                                  │     │
│     └──────────────────────────────────┘     │
│        ▲                                     │
│        └── BLUE CROPPING BOUNDARY BOX        │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### How to Set the Focal Point:
1. Whenever you upload an image (in Artworks, Public Art, Press, or Contact), hover over the image preview and click **Edit** (or click directly on the image thumbnail).
2. You will see your photo with a circular target (the **Hotspot dot**) and a blue rectangle (the **Crop box**).
3. **Move the Hotspot Circle**: Click and drag the small circular dot directly onto the most important part of the image — usually the subject's face or eyes in figurative works.
4. **Adjust the Crop Box**: If the original photo has excess background or studio clutter around the canvas, drag the corners of the outer blue box inward to frame the artwork cleanly.
5. Click **Close** in the top corner of the dialog.
6. Click **Publish**.

> **What this does:** When your website displays this image on a vertical phone screen, it will automatically center around your hotspot dot, ensuring your subject is always framed with gallery-quality perfection!

## 6. Managing Public Art & Murals

Your large-scale architectural projects, community murals, and public installations have a dedicated home under **Public Art Projects**.

### Adding a Public Art Project:
1. Click **Public Art Project** in the left menu.
2. Click the `+` icon to create a new project.
3. Complete the project fields:
   - **Project Title**: (e.g., *"Echoes of Heritage — Civic Center Mural"*).
   - **Slug**: Click **Generate**.
   - **Medium**: (e.g., *"Exterior weather-resistant acrylic and mineral silicate paint on primed masonry"*).
   - **Location / Venue**: Street or building name (e.g., *"Cultural Quarter Gateway"*).
   - **City & Country**: (e.g., *"Leicester"*, *"United Kingdom"*).
   - **Year**: Completion year (e.g., `2025`).
   - **Commissioning Body / Organizer**: The council, gallery, or sponsor that funded the project (e.g., *"British Council & Leicester City Council"*).
   - **Dimensions / Scale**: Physical size of the wall (e.g., *"12 × 4 meters (48 sq meters)"*).
   - **Impact Metric / Callout**: A brief headline statistic or achievement (e.g., *"Over 25,000 daily civic commuters"* or *"Shortlisted for Public Art Award"*).
   - **Description**: Comprehensive story of the mural, community engagement, and cultural narrative.
   - **Project Images Gallery**: You can upload **multiple** photos here! Show wide architectural views, aerial perspectives, and close-up detail brushwork. Drag to reorder photos (the first image serves as the main cover).
   - **External Link**: (Optional) Link to a press release, council publication, or video documentary.
4. Click **Publish**.

## 7. Adding Exhibitions, Recognitions & Press

Keep your biographical timeline, press clippings, and global exhibition record completely up-to-date.

### A. Exhibitions
1. Click **Exhibitions** in the left menu.
2. Click `+` to add an exhibition.
3. Fill in:
   - **Title**: (e.g., *"Silent Dialogues: Modern Figurative Perspectives"*).
   - **Subtitle**: (e.g., *"Solo Exhibition"* or *"Curated Group Show"*).
   - **Status**: Choose **Upcoming**, **Ongoing**, or **Past**. Upcoming exhibitions automatically appear in announcement banners.
   - **Date / Period**: (e.g., *"14 October – 28 November 2026"*).
   - **Venue, City, & Country**: (e.g., *"Somerset House"*, *"London"*, *"United Kingdom"*).
   - **Artist Role & Curator**: (e.g., *"Lead Exhibiting Artist"*, Curator: *"Dr. Elena Rossi"*).
   - **Exhibition Image**: Upload the official poster, gallery catalogue cover, or installation view. Use the Hotspot tool to center it.
   - **Exhibition Highlights**: Add key bullet points (e.g., *"12 new large-scale canvas works exhibited"*, *"Artist talk and catalogue launch"*).
4. Click **Publish**.

### B. Recognitions & Awards
1. Click **Recognition & Award Item** in the left navigation.
2. Enter the **Award / Selection Title** (e.g., *"British Council International Visual Arts Fellowship"*).
3. Enter the **Awarding Body** (e.g., *"British Council & Arts Council England"*).
4. Select the **Status**: *Selected*, *Won*, *Nominated*, *Shortlisted*, or *Featured*.
5. **Highlight on Home Page**: Toggle this switch to **On** if you want this award to be highlighted on the front page of your website.
6. Click **Publish**.

### C. Press & Media Articles
1. Click **Press & Media** in the left navigation.
2. Enter the **Article Title**, **Publication Name** (e.g., *"Artforum"* or *"The Guardian"*), and publication **Date**.
3. Upload an **Article Scan / Press Cover Photo**.
4. Paste the **Article Link (URL)** so readers can read the full piece online.
5. Toggle **Highlight on Home Page** if you want this interview or review to appear on your homepage.
6. Click **Publish**.

## 9. Publishing, Discarding Changes & Safe Editing

Sanity has built-in safety features designed to prevent accidental mistakes or loss of work.

### Drafts vs. Published
- When you type into any field, Sanity saves your changes **automatically** as a private **Draft**.
- A small yellow badge labeled **"Draft"** appears in the bottom status bar.
- Visitors to your public website cannot see your draft until you click **Publish**.

### Discarding Unwanted Changes
- If you made changes to a document and change your mind, click the small down-arrow or three dots (`...`) next to the Publish button.
- Select **Discard changes**.
- The document will revert back to its last published version.

### Safe Singletons
- Core pages like your *Home Page*, *About*, *CV*, and *Contact* are configured as protected **Singletons**.
- They cannot be accidentally deleted or duplicated, keeping your website structure safe and stable at all times.

## 10. Image Preparation & Pro Tips

To ensure your artwork looks vibrant and loads rapidly for gallerists, curators, and collectors across the world, follow these simple photography tips:

| Aspect | Recommendation | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **File Formats** | **JPG**, **PNG**, or **WebP** | JPG is best for paintings; PNG is great for drawings with fine line detail. |
| **Image Resolution** | **2000px to 3500px** on the longest side | Large enough for crisp detail viewing on 4K retina screens without being excessively heavy. |
| **Color Space** | **sRGB** | Standard web color profile that ensures colors look identical across iPhones, Macs, and PC monitors. |
| **File Size** | **Between 1MB and 8MB** | The website automatically compresses and formats images for fast mobile loading, but keeping originals under 10MB ensures swift uploads. |
| **Lighting** | Even, glare-free indirect daylight | Avoid harsh direct camera flashes that create reflections on varnish or oil paint texture. |
